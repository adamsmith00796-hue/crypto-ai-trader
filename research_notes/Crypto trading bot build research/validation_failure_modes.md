# Validating a Crypto Trading Bot, and Why Retail/"AI" Bots Fail

Context: researched 24 Sep 2026 for an Australian user with about US$200 to trade. Research budget was about 18 tool calls. Sources are ranked in this order: academic/regulator first, open-source tool docs second, practitioner blogs last.

## 1. Proper backtesting mechanics (lookahead, fills, fees, walk-forward, out-of-sample, Monte Carlo, trade counts)

### Takeaway
Most "great" backtests are broken by how they were built before any statistics come in: signals that peek at the current candle, fills that assume no slippage, stops and targets that resolve in the best order inside a candle, and costs that are missing. Open-source engines like Freqtrade say outright that backtests rest on simplifying assumptions and never replace dry-run (paper) trading.

### Cited Findings
- Freqtrade explains that strategies can "cheat" because backtesting fills in the full dataframe, every candle timestamp included, at the outset, so careless code can look into the future. It ships a `lookahead-analysis` command that chains backtests to provoke lookahead bias, and a `recursive-analysis` command that checks whether indicator values change with the amount of warm-up data (`startup_candle_count`). The docs say to run both before dry or live mode. — [Freqtrade Lookahead analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/); [Freqtrade Recursive analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/recursive-analysis/)
- Freqtrade's stated backtest assumptions: signals are generated on candle close and trades start at the next candle's open. It assumes "all orders are filled at the requested price (no slippage) as long as the price is within the candle's high/low range". When a stoploss and an ROI target could both trigger within one candle, the stoploss is evaluated first. Intra-candle price order is unknown, which creates bias that can only be reduced with `--timeframe-detail`, i.e. using lower-timeframe data. — [Freqtrade Backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)
- Freqtrade includes exchange default fees in profit calculations, and custom fees can be set with `--fee`. It warns that historic trading limits and precision values are unavailable, so current limits are applied, and that dynamic pairlists make results unreproducible. It states backtesting "will never replace running a strategy in dry-run mode." — [Freqtrade Backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)
- Walk-forward optimisation re-optimises on rolling in-sample windows and tests on the next out-of-sample window. In a stable system each window picks similar parameter values. Wildly different parameters from fold to fold show the optimiser is chasing noise. Practitioner rule of thumb for walk-forward efficiency (out-of-sample vs in-sample performance): above 0.5 is acceptable, above 0.7 is strong, below 0.3 is likely overfit. Practitioner source only, not academic. — [QuanterLab: Interpreting Walk-Forward Results](https://quanterlab.com/articles/cookbook-interpreting-walkforward) (via search summary); [ClearEdge Trading WFO](https://clearedge.trading/post/walk-forward-optimization-futures-strategy-validation)
- Bailey et al. argue that standard hold-out techniques "tend to be unreliable and inaccurate" for investment backtests. A single hold-out split is not enough, because once a researcher has looked at the hold-out and gone back to tweak, it is no longer out of sample. — [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting", SSRN 2326253 / J. Computational Finance 2015](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253)
- A May 2026 study of machine-learning Bitcoin strategies used about 70,000 hourly BTC-USDT bars (2018–2026) and 27 walk-forward folds at 10 bp per trade. Naive sign-based ML strategies **lost money after 10 bp costs**. Only a cost-aware filter that skipped small predicted moves and cut turnover restored profitability "in selected configurations". Reviewers flagged that it was unclear whether the filter threshold was fixed in advance or tuned on performance, which is itself a leakage risk. — [Bysik & Ślepaczuk, "Machine Learning-Based Bitcoin Trading Under Transaction Costs" (arXiv 2606.00060, via Pith)](https://pith.science/paper/2606.00060)
- A study of 75,360 simple technical rules on Bitcoin used a multiple-hypothesis procedure to select the best rules after transaction costs, then tested out of sample. It reports that selected rules can beat buy-and-hold, "especially risk-return wise". This is a counter-point: some edge in simple trend rules may exist, but only after proper data-snooping correction. — [Frömmel & Deprez, "Are simple technical trading rules profitable in bitcoin markets?", Int. Review of Economics & Finance 2024 (search summary; full text 403)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056024003010)
- White's Reality Check (bootstrap) tests the best rule against the whole universe of rules tried, to measure data-snooping bias. It is used in crypto trading-rule studies. — [Gerritsen et al. 2020, "The profitability of technical trading rules in the Bitcoin market"](http://dirkgerritsen.nl/uploads/gerritsen_et_al_2020_bitcoin_trading_rules.pdf); [Reality check on ML vs TA in crypto, Finance Research Letters](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612320304414)

### Inferences
- Minimum mechanical checklist for any backtest: (1) signals use closed candles only, and entries happen at the next bar's open or later; (2) run Freqtrade `lookahead-analysis` and `recursive-analysis`, or the equivalent, and fix any flags; (3) use `--timeframe-detail` (for example, 1-minute data under 1-hour candles) whenever stops or targets sit inside bars; (4) use taker fees, not maker fees, unless orders really rest on the book, and add slippage of at least 0.05–0.1% per side for liquid majors and more for small caps; (5) include perpetual funding if futures are used; (6) test on pairs that existed at the time, including delisted coins, to avoid survivorship bias.
- For a $200 account, fee drag is the dominant cost. At roughly 0.1% taker per side (0.2% round trip), one round trip a day costs about 73% of capital per year in fees before slippage (365 × 0.2%). A strategy needs a large edge per trade, or very low turnover, just to break even. This is arithmetic, not a sourced statistic, and the exact fee depends on the exchange tier.
- Monte Carlo trade reshuffling and bootstrapping (resampling the sequence of trade returns thousands of times) gives a distribution of max drawdown and final equity, not one lucky path. Plan capital around the 95th-percentile drawdown, not the backtested one. No primary source was fetched for a specific threshold (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found for a **minimum trade count** threshold. The common practitioner rule of thumb is 100+ trades, preferably 200–300+, across several market regimes, but I could not verify it against a primary source, so treat it as heuristic. Statistically, the standard error of a mean trade return shrinks with √n, so 30 trades is almost never enough to separate a small edge from zero.
- No primary source retrieved for a specific Monte Carlo reshuffle protocol or threshold.
- Survivorship bias in crypto (delisted coins missing from exchange data) was not covered by a fetched source. It is inferred from general backtesting principles.

## 2. Overfitting: PBO, Deflated Sharpe Ratio, "Pseudo-mathematics", how many parameter combos is too many

### Takeaway
The more strategy variants you try, the higher the best backtest Sharpe will be by pure luck. The best in-sample configuration's expected out-of-sample rank falls sharply as the number of configurations grows. Any reported Sharpe must be deflated by the number of trials, and most retail backtests never report that number.

### Cited Findings
- PBO paper: "the expected out-of-sample performance rank of the best in-sample configuration declines sharply with the number of configurations tested". It proposes Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV) to estimate the probability that a backtest is overfit. — [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253); [author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)
- An open-source Python implementation of PBO/CSCV exists. — [pypbo on GitHub](https://github.com/esvhd/pypbo)
- "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" (Notices of the AMS, May 2014): high simulated performance is "easily achievable after backtesting a relatively small number of alternative strategy configurations". Researchers rarely report how many configurations they tried, so investors cannot judge overfitting. **Under memory effects (serial dependence), overfitting leads to negative expected out-of-sample returns, not just zero.** It introduces the Minimum Backtest Length (MinBTL) concept. — [SSRN 2308659](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2308659); [AMS Notices PDF](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf); [phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2014-04-pseudo-mathematics-financial-charlatanism.html)
- MinBTL headline number from the paper, **not re-verified this session because the AMS PDF returned 403, so confirm before quoting**: with about 5 years of daily data, testing more than about 45 independent strategy configurations is enough to expect at least one to show an in-sample annualised Sharpe of about 1.0 when the true Sharpe is zero. The approximate formula is MinBTL ≈ 2·ln(N) / E[max SR]². — [AMS Notices PDF](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf) (to verify)
- The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) corrects for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns (skew, kurtosis), and accounts for sample length. It builds on the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR, 2012), which gives the probability that the true SR exceeds a threshold. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551); [Wikipedia: Deflated Sharpe ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio); [author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)
  - Caution for the report writer: an automated summary of the DSR PDF gave formulas ("DSR = SR × [1 − γ ln N/√T]") that do **not** match the paper's actual construction. The real DSR is PSR evaluated against a benchmark equal to the expected maximum SR across N trials. Do not quote that summary's formulas or its numeric example.
- Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting" (J. Portfolio Management 2015): the multiple-testing "haircut" to a Sharpe ratio is **nonlinear**. The highest Sharpes are moderately penalised and marginal Sharpes heavily penalised, so a flat 50% haircut rule of thumb is a "serious mistake". Harvey, Liu & Zhu recommend a t-stat hurdle of about 3.0 instead of 2.0 for new factors. — [Harvey & Liu, Backtesting (CME-hosted PDF)](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/backtesting.pdf); [Duke practical applications PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Media/2016/Practical_applications_backtesting.pdf); [Man Group summary](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting)
- Robot Wealth (practitioner): "In the majority of cases optimisation is your enemy." Pick a stable plateau region, not an isolated peak, because a lower but stable region "implies a degree of robustness". Genetic optimisers "zero in on anything" that helps the objective, noise included, so a bigger search space means more overfitting. — [Robot Wealth, Parameter Optimisation for Systematic Trading](https://robotwealth.com/parameter-optimisation-for-systematic-trading/)

### Inferences
- Practical rule: **log every variant tried**, including indicator swaps, timeframes, pairs and stop levels. With a few years of crypto data, a few dozen genuinely different variants is already enough to produce a Sharpe-1 mirage. A 3-parameter grid of 10×10×10 = 1,000 combos is far past that. Parameters are correlated, so the effective N is lower than the raw count, but still large.
- Parameter-stability test: sweep each parameter ±20–30% around the chosen value. If performance collapses, the result is a fitted spike. Plot the heatmap and choose the centre of a plateau.
- Report PBO (via CSCV) and DSR for the final candidate. Treat DSR under 0.95, i.e. less than 95% probability the true SR is above zero after deflation, as not proven.
- Fewer rules and parameters is better. Each added filter adds a degree of freedom that the backtest will exploit.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the full DSR or MinBTL PDFs (403 / tool summarisation unreliable). Exact formula statements and the "45 trials" example should be checked against the original PDF.

## 3. Benchmarks and risk-adjusted metrics

### Takeaway
A crypto bot must beat simply holding BTC on a risk-adjusted basis, and a trivial trend filter such as "hold BTC only when above its 200-day MA", after costs. Independent tests of popular retail signal families find none clearly beats appropriate benchmarks, and simple trend and momentum rules are the only families not refuted.

### Cited Findings
- "Retail Trader's Ruin: An Anatomy of Popular Signal Failure" (Darmanin, arXiv, July 2026) tested five heavily promoted retail signal families plus a momentum control. It used exposure-matched benchmarks, stationary-bootstrap confidence intervals, hierarchical multiple-comparison corrections, trading costs, and FINRA/ESMA leverage scenarios. Result: **oscillator, volume, calendar and candlestick signals were REFUTED; trend and momentum were INCONCLUSIVE (wide CIs); none was SUPPORTED.** — [arXiv 2607.20093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20093)
- Frömmel & Deprez report that simple technical rules selected with multiple-testing control can outperform buy-and-hold in Bitcoin out of sample, especially on a risk-adjusted basis. This supports the value of a simple trend filter as a benchmark and baseline. — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056024003010)
- The Deflated Sharpe framework shows that Sharpe estimates also depend on non-normality (skew, kurtosis) and sample length. Crypto returns are fat-tailed, so raw Sharpe overstates reliability. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)

### Inferences
- Minimum benchmark set: (a) buy-and-hold BTC; (b) the same, with a 200-day or 50/200 MA filter; (c) the bot. Compare over the same period and after the same costs. If the bot does not beat (b) on Calmar (CAGR ÷ max drawdown) and Sortino, it is not worth the complexity.
- Metrics to report: CAGR, annualised Sharpe (use 365-day annualisation for crypto), Sortino (downside deviation only), Calmar, max drawdown and its duration, win rate with average win/loss, profit factor, exposure % (time in market), trade count, and fees paid as a % of gross profit.
- An "exposure-matched" comparison matters. A bot in the market only 30% of the time should be compared with a 30%-exposure buy-and-hold, not 100%.

### Gaps
- No independent source fetched with specific numeric thresholds, e.g. "Sharpe > 1 required". Thresholds in the protocol are heuristic.

## 4. Paper-trading and forward-testing: duration, what to measure, going live, scaling

### Takeaway
Forward testing (dry-run) is required, but it mainly catches implementation errors, cost and fill mismatches, and lookahead bugs. It is too short to prove an edge statistically. Live scaling should be staged and tied to pre-committed kill criteria.

### Cited Findings
- Freqtrade: backtesting "will never replace running a strategy in dry-run mode". Past results do not guarantee future success. — [Freqtrade Backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)
- Bailey et al.: hold-out alone is unreliable, and overfit strategies can have negative expected out-of-sample returns. A live or paper period that underperforms the backtest is the expected symptom of overfitting, not bad luck. — [SSRN 2308659](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2308659)

### Inferences (heuristic protocol; no primary source found for exact durations)
- Paper-trade for at least 4–8 weeks **and** at least 30–50 trades, whichever is later, ideally spanning a volatility spike. Measure: (1) live signals match the backtest re-run over the same period (signal parity, the key lookahead detector); (2) realised fill price vs candle price (slippage); (3) fees; (4) API errors and missed orders; (5) P&L vs benchmark.
- Go-live gate: the paper period sits within the Monte Carlo 5th–95th percentile band of the backtest, signal parity is at least 95%, and there are no unhandled errors.
- Scaling for a $200 account: start at the exchange minimum order size, grow only after each block of about 30 live trades stays within expected bands, and never add capital to "win it back". Pre-commit a kill switch, e.g. halt if live drawdown exceeds 1.5× the backtested max drawdown, or if rolling performance falls below the benchmark for a set period.
- With $200, the money is best treated as tuition. The main value of live trading is learning the operational failures, not profit.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found giving a specific paper-trading duration or scaling rule. The figures above are practitioner convention and my own reasoning.

## 5. Failure modes with evidence and real examples

### Takeaway
Bots fail mostly for boring reasons: overfit to a past regime, eaten by fees, exposed to leverage and exchange failure in tail events, or the underlying signal never had an edge. The October 2025 crash showed exchange and API failure arriving exactly when bots need to act.

### Cited Findings
- **Leverage liquidation and exchange outage (10 Oct 2025):** about US$19B of leveraged positions were liquidated and about 1.6 million accounts, the largest liquidation event in crypto history. BTC fell about 14.5% after a tariff announcement at 14:57 UTC. Binance reported "systems under heavy load" with API failures. dYdX was offline for about 8 hours and Lighter for about 4.5 hours, so traders could not manage positions or add collateral. USDe depegged to about $0.65 on Binance. — [CoinGecko explainer](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2025/10/13/cryptos-black-friday-inside-the-19-billion-market-meltdown/); [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity); [CoinShares](https://coinshares.com/us/insights/knowledge/billions-in-liquidations-what-happened/)
- **Fee drag and overtrading:** naive ML BTC strategies on hourly data lost money at 10 bp per trade, and only turnover-cutting filters made them profitable. — [Bysik & Ślepaczuk 2026](https://pith.science/paper/2606.00060)
- **Signals without edge:** oscillator (RSI/stochastic-type), volume, calendar and candlestick signals, the staple of YouTube and influencer strategies, were refuted after costs and multiple-testing correction. — [arXiv 2607.20093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20093)
- **Over-optimisation:** overfit backtests can produce negative out-of-sample expected returns. — [SSRN 2308659](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2308659)
- **Backtest engine illusions:** fills assumed at requested price with no slippage, and unknown intra-candle ordering. — [Freqtrade](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)
- **No learning, and persistence does not help (human traders):** see section 6. The Brazil and Taiwan data show experience does not turn losing traders into winners.

### Inferences
- Regime change: a strategy tuned on the 2020–21 or 2024 bull runs, which were mostly trend-following gains, will usually bleed in chop (2022 bear, 2023 range). Always report results split by regime: bull, bear, sideways.
- Strategy decay: published or popular edges get arbitraged away. If an edge is on YouTube, assume it is crowded.
- Operational: API key permissions (never enable withdrawals), rate limits, partial fills, exchange maintenance windows, stale websocket data, clock drift and silent crashes all need handling. The bot must fail safe, i.e. flat or with protective stops sitting on the exchange, not only in the bot's memory.
- Leverage: with $200, even 3–5× leverage means one October-2025-type candle can liquidate the account, and exchange outages can stop the bot reacting. Recommend spot only.

### Gaps
- No systematic dataset found on the live performance of retail crypto bots (e.g. 3Commas, Pionex or Cryptohopper user outcomes). Platforms do not publish aggregate user P&L. The 2022 3Commas API-key leak is relevant but was not researched in this pass.
- Strategy-decay evidence specific to crypto was not fetched.

## 6. Evidence on retail trader, bot and signal-group outcomes; regulator warnings (ASIC, ACCC, CFTC)

### Takeaway
Large-sample studies show day traders almost universally lose after costs, and fewer than 1% are predictably profitable. Regulators, ASIC included in 2026, name "AI trading bots" promising passive income as a signature investment-scam narrative. Influencer-sold bots and signal groups are a major scam channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Brazil (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti):** all 19,646 individuals who began day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013–2015 were studied. Of those who persisted 300+ days (1,551 people), **97% lost money**, only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage, and 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary. The top individual averaged US$310/day with a standard deviation of US$2,560. The share profitable falls with persistence: 29.8% of one-day traders, 15.5% at 2–50 days, 3.0% at 300+ days. There was no evidence of learning. It is "virtually impossible" to day trade for a living, contrary to course providers' claims. — [SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)
- **Taiwan (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, and Zhang in "Learning, Fast or Slow?"):** 1992–2006, about 3.7B trades. **Fewer than 1% of day traders are predictably profitable.** Day traders lost on average 23.9 bp/day net of fees. Aggregate performance was negative in 14 of 15 years. More than 75% quit within two years. — [Learning, Fast or Slow?, Review of Asset Pricing Studies 2020](https://academic.oup.com/raps/article/10/1/61/5545095); [SSRN 2535636](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2535636)
- In "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill", the top 500 ranked Taiwanese day traders earned 61.3 bp/day before fees and 37.9 bp after, and bottom-ranked traders earned −11.5 bp before and −28.9 bp after. A small skilled minority exists, but most lose, and fees make things worse. — [SSRN 529063](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=529063); [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- **ASIC (media release 26-063MR, 2026):** in calendar 2025 ASIC coordinated removal of 11,964 phishing and investment scam websites, up 90% on the prior 12 months (6,270), or about 32 a day. It has removed more than 25,000 since 2023, plus more than 1,100 social-media investment scam ads in 2025. Australians lost $2.18B to scams in 2025, $837.7M of it to investment scams. Named tactics: "AI trading bots claiming to generate passive income", AI-made fake celebrity endorsements, fake ASIC registration certificates, and cloaking to evade platform detection. Commissioner Alan Kirkland: scammers use AI to make fake investment ads "more polished, more convincing and harder to spot". — [ASIC 26-063MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-063mr-asic-ramps-up-action-to-protect-consumers-from-ai-powered-online-investment-scams)
- **ASIC signal-group warning:** "share trading" and "stock tips" messaging-app groups are used to push victims onto fake crypto trading platforms that show fabricated trades and profits, while deposits go straight to the scammers. — [ASIC scam alert: fake crypto-asset trading platforms](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/scam-alert-scammers-luring-investors-onto-fake-crypto-asset-trading-platforms)
- **Moneysmart:** a walk-through of an "AI investment scam journey", and guidance to check finfluencers against ASIC's professional register. — [Moneysmart: An AI investment scam journey](https://moneysmart.gov.au/financial-scams/an-ai-investment-scam-journey); [Moneysmart: What is a finfluencer](https://moneysmart.gov.au/online-safety/what-is-a-finfluencer)
- **ASIC finfluencer crackdown (26-081MR, 2026):** warning notices issued to four finfluencers suspected of unlicensed advice, 15 authorised-representative finfluencers reviewed, joint action with 16 other regulators in a Global Week of Action. Kirkland: "If someone on social media is promising easy money or guaranteed returns, there is a real risk they're breaking the law." — [ASIC 26-081MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-081mr-asic-continues-finfluencer-crackdown-alongside-global-regulators/)
- Moneysmart's Gen Z research, as reported by media: about 23% of Gen Z hold crypto, and 29% said they trade short-term based on influencers. These are secondary reports; the primary Moneysmart study was not fetched. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/asic-warns-ai-finfluencers-gen-z-crypto-23-percent)
- **CFTC customer advisory (25 Jan 2024):** AI trading-bot scams promise things like 10% a month, returns in "the tens of thousands of percent" and "100 percent win rates", and are promoted by social-media influencers. AI cannot predict future market movements. Example: Mirror Trading International (Cornelius Steynberg) took more than US$1.7B in bitcoin from at least 23,000 people. It was a Ponzi scheme using fake MetaTrader demo accounts, promoted on Facebook, Instagram and YouTube. — [CFTC Press Release 8854-24](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8854-24)
- **SEC v. Nathan Fuller (Privvy):** an alleged $12.3M crypto scheme built around fake AI trading bots and guaranteed returns. The SEC seeks injunctions, disgorgement and penalties. Secondary news source. — [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/sec-privvy-fuller-ai-bot-fraud/); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/the-secs-latest-crypto-fraud-case-shows-ai-hype-still-sells/)

### Inferences
- Red-flag checklist for any bot or signal product: guaranteed or monthly-percentage returns; win rates near 100%; no verifiable, exchange-linked, multi-year live track record; returns shown as screenshots or on the vendor's own platform; paying for "AI" access or upgrades; requests for API keys with withdrawal rights or deposits to the vendor's platform; urgency; celebrity endorsements; unlicensed in Australia (check the ASIC register).
- A vendor selling a bot that really works has a strong incentive to trade it, not sell it. Selling it only makes sense if the edge is capacity-limited, which a public mass-market bot cannot be, or if the product is the subscription itself.
- Legitimate open-source tools (Freqtrade, Jesse) carry no return claims and explicitly warn about backtest limits. That contrast is itself a useful signal.
