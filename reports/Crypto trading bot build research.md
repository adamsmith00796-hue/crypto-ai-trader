# Rebuild the six dots for slow, cheap trades

**The short answer:** you can build a self-running BTC/USDT bot in the style of the six-dot app, but only if it trades slowly, rarely and cheaply. Your own test already showed what happens otherwise: the 15-minute version lost the whole $200 to fees, and the 1-hour version lost 30 to 50%. The research agrees. The only kind of signal with solid evidence behind it on Bitcoin is a slow trend filter on daily candles, "hold when the trend is up, sit in cash when it is not". Even that mostly protects you from big crashes rather than beating simply buying and holding. The best build is a long-only (buy, never short) spot bot on daily candles, using 4-hour candles only to time entries. Its six dots should each measure something different, instead of six versions of the same momentum reading. It should exit with a trailing stop, not a TP1/TP2/TP3 ladder, and run on Freqtrade, a free open-source bot program, connected to a low-fee exchange such as Binance spot. Before any real money goes in, it should pass four gates: a clean backtest, a fair comparison against simple benchmarks, a test on data it was never tuned on, and 4 to 8 weeks of paper trading. Be realistic about the money. A good result on $200 is losing less than buy-and-hold in a crash and ending within a few dollars of it in a boom. So treat the $200 as the cost of learning, not as income.

## Your backtest failed exactly where the research says bots fail

Your two-year test (EMA200, 4-hour EMA50, MACD, RSI above 50, OBV, and DI/ADX) went wrong in three ways the research predicts. The first is fees. At about 0.1% per side on a low-cost exchange, one round trip a day costs roughly **73% of the account per year** before slippage (365 × 0.2%). A 15-minute system making 50 to 150 trades a month would need a gross edge of 5 to 15% a month just to pay its fees, and no credible evidence shows an edge that large. Mean-reversion tests on BTC and ETH across four timeframes averaged **-14.4% on 15-minute, -8.1% on 1-hour, -9.5% on 4-hour and -4.0% on daily candles** after fees, and one 15-minute Bollinger test paid about $2,300 in fees on a $10,000 account ([Coinquant](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/building-a-mean-reversion-strategy-in-cryptocurrency-markets-evidence-from-78-backtests)). An independent 2026 study of hourly BTC models found that a naive 24-hour momentum rule **lost 45.93% over 5,984 trades at 10 basis points (0.1%) of cost**. The same study found that forecasts only became useful when a filter cut trading by more than 90% ([Bysik & Ślepaczuk, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00060)). Your result, small gains with zero fees and heavy losses with fees, is the classic pattern: the signal was too weak to pay its trading costs.

The second problem is that your six dots were mostly one dot counted several times. EMA200, EMA50, MACD, RSI and ADX all come from recent price movement. Requiring them all to agree is close to one momentum signal with a stricter threshold, plus five extra settings that a backtest can over-tune. OBV adds volume, but a July 2026 study of the signal types that retail traders and YouTube promote tested them properly, with trading costs and corrections for testing many rules at once. It found **oscillator, volume, calendar and candlestick signals were refuted, trend and momentum were inconclusive, and none was supported** ([Darmanin, arXiv 2607.20093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20093)). No academic study was found that tests "N indicators must agree" rules on crypto directly. The indirect evidence says confluence helps mainly because it cuts the number of trades, which lowers fees. It does not help by adding insight.

The third problem is the benchmark. Buy-and-hold turned $200 into $260 over your test window and beat every version. That is normal. The largest multiple-testing study of about 15,000 technical rules found real profitability in crypto overall, but **"no predictability for Bitcoin in the out-of-sample period"** ([Hudson & Urquhart, Annals of Operations Research](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Technical-trading-and-cryptocurrencies-Hudson-Urquhart/f5b07a2ef27fc544fff9ae800f508e7cf0a36941)). A moving-average rule earned **8.76% a year of excess return, but only when Bitcoin was excluded** ([Grobys, Ahmed & Sapkota, Finance Research Letters](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v32y2020ics1544612319308852.html)). BTC is the hardest coin to beat with simple chart rules.

## Six dots that measure different things, computed on daily candles

The one family of signals that holds up after realistic fees is **slow, long-only trend following**. A careful 2023 study charged 15 basis points (0.15%) per trade. It found that holding the crypto market only when its 28-day return ranked in the top third of its history gave a **Sharpe ratio (return per unit of risk) of 1.51 versus 0.85 for buy-and-hold, while invested only 48% of the time**. It also had lower drawdowns (fall from peak) than the market in every year. Two warnings came with it. **Short-selling on the same signals lost money in almost every configuration even before costs**, and the authors call their own best settings "an optimistic view" because they were picked with hindsight ([Han, Kang & Ryu, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4675565); [full PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). Separately, the ratio of the BTC price to its 5- to 100-day moving averages predicted daily returns both in-sample and out-of-sample, although that sample ends around 2018 ([Detzel et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3115846)). Practitioners protect against lucky settings by averaging a Donchian channel breakout (a new high over a set number of days) across several lookback lengths. They report a Sharpe above 1.5 after fees, but they do not publish the fee level ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/)).

This points to a panel where three dots carry the direction decision and each uses different information. Dot 1 is the **daily trend**: the daily close is above its 100-day moving average. Dot 2 is **28-day momentum**: BTC's return over the last 28 days is positive. Dot 3 is a **breakout ensemble**: price sits in the upper half of its 20-, 55- and 100-day Donchian channels on at least two of the three. Dot 4 is a **slow regime check**: the weekly close is above its 20-week average. This dot could instead use the on-chain valuation measure MVRV Z-score, which, when kept out of its "euphoric" zone, gave a **Sharpe of 1.28 versus 0.45 for buy-and-hold from 2013 to 2025**. That result rests on only about three entries and three exits, however, and its main tables ignore costs ([Grobys, Näsman & Sandretto, open-access PDF](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/6575b90c-6140-44e7-a798-011a1793d134/content)). Dot 5 is a **4-hour pullback timer**: 4-hour RSI has dipped below about 40 and turned back up while dots 1 to 3 are green. The evidence for this is weak. It belongs only as a timing aid inside an uptrend, never as a reason to buy on its own. Dot 6 is a **volatility check**: daily ATR (average daily price range) as a percentage of price is not in its top tenth of the past year. Volatility has good support as a way to size trades, and much of the benefit credited to crypto trend systems actually comes from volatility control ([Marrazzo summary](https://antoniomarrazzo.substack.com/p/trend-following-mostly-a-volatility)).

Two popular inputs should stay out. The Fear & Greed Index follows price rather than leading it, and it gives **no out-of-sample forecasting gain** for daily BTC returns ([ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S305070062600006X)). Funding rates on futures are themselves predictable, but no rigorous study shows they predict BTC's direction after costs ([Inan, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5576424)). If you want funding in the panel at all, use it only as a "skip this buy" veto when it is extreme, and treat it as unproven.

**Daily candles should decide direction, and 4-hour candles only time the entry.** Adding a daily trend filter to hourly BTC signals cut false signals and trade count, and improved return relative to risk ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-to-design-a-simple-multi-timeframe-trend-strategy-on-bitcoin/)). A plain EMA 21/55 trend rule on 15-minute BTC candles lost 6.5% in backtest ([Coinquant](https://www.coinquant.ai/strategies/btc-trend-following-15m-backtest)). Australia's regulator found that among retail traders placing more than 50 trades a month, **only 19% were profitable after fees, against 32% overall** ([ASIC 26-004MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-004mr-asic-secures-nearly-40-million-in-refunds-to-investors-and-drives-change-after-cfd-sector-falls-short/)). Aim for fewer than 10 round trips a month. A daily system will usually make fewer than that.

## One entry, one exit and a trailing stop, instead of TP1/TP2/TP3

The strongest rigorous work on exits shows that **stop-losses only add value when prices trend**. When prices move randomly, stops always lower expected return. When they trend, the benefit of a stop grows with the strength of the trend ([Kaminski & Lo, MIT PDF](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). A trend bot therefore needs a stop that trails behind price and lets big winners run. A fixed ladder of profit targets cuts off the rare large gains that trend following depends on. It also triples the number of orders, each paying a fee. On a $200 account, a $60 to $100 position split into thirds also pushes each piece down towards the exchange's minimum order size, which was not confirmed for any exchange here. Moving the stop to breakeven early looks tidy, but a "breakeven" exit still pays two fees, so it is really a small loss. No head-to-head crypto study of trailing stops, profit ladders and time stops was found, so your own out-of-sample testing has to settle the exact choice.

**The recommended exit:** a starting stop at 2.5 to 3 times daily ATR below the entry, which then trails up as price rises and never moves down. The bot also sells everything when two of the three core trend dots (1 to 3) turn red. This replaces "4 or more red = sell" with a rule tied to the dots that carry the evidence. You can still show all six dots on your dashboard, since they are useful for watching the bot.

**Position sizing** follows from that stop. Size each trade so that hitting the stop loses no more than 1 to 2% of the account. Position size equals the dollars at risk divided by the stop distance. With $200 at 1% risk and a 5% stop, the position is $40. With a 2% stop it is $100. This arithmetic is our own, not from a source. Wide daily stops keep positions small and leave most of the account in cash. That is intended, because it keeps fees low as a share of each trade. Full Kelly sizing (a formula that bets a set share of the account based on estimated win odds) is too aggressive when a backtest's win rate is this uncertain.

**Leverage should be zero.** ASIC found that **68% of retail CFD clients lost money** and caps retail crypto CFD leverage at 2:1 ([ASIC REP 828](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tq0he35c/rep828-published-20-january-2026.pdf)). On 10 October 2025, about **US$19 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated across roughly 1.6 million accounts**, BTC fell about 14.5%, and Binance reported API failures under heavy load ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)). A spot-only bot cannot be liquidated.

## Freqtrade on a low-fee spot exchange is the only setup where the fees add up

**Freqtrade is the right software.** It is free, open source, written in Python, the most-starred bot project on GitHub (54,737 stars), and updated monthly (release 2026.8 on 31 August 2026). It includes paper trading (called dry-run), Telegram alerts, and a web API your FastAPI and Next.js dashboard can read ([GitHub](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade); [Freqtrade REST API docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/rest-api/)). Its most valuable feature for you is a built-in check for **lookahead bias**, the most common way indicator backtests fool people: code that quietly uses candle data that would not have existed yet at the moment of the decision ([Freqtrade lookahead-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/)). The alternatives fit worse. Jesse's live trading is a paid add-on listed at **US$1,600**, eight times your whole account ([Jesse blog](https://jesse.trade/blog/news/black-friday-sale-for-the-premium-live-trade-plugin)). Hummingbot and NautilusTrader are built for professional high-speed trading. Backtrader has not been updated since August 2024. TradingView webhook services cost US$10 to US$50 a month or more, which is 5 to 25% of the account every month ([TradersPost](https://traderspost.io/compare/traderspost-vs-3commas)).

**Exchange fees decide which exchanges are possible.** Binance charges **0.10% per side on spot** ([Binance fees](https://www.binance.com/en-AU/fee/trading)). BTC Markets lists **0.20% for orders that fill straight away (taker) and a 0.05% rebate for orders that wait on the order book (maker)**, but the date of that page is unknown ([BTC Markets support](https://support.btcmarkets.net/hc/en-us/articles/360038037153-Bitcoin-Markets-Maker-Taker-Fee-Model)). Swyftx charges 0.6% per side ([cryptonews.com.au](https://cryptonews.com.au/guides/swyftx-vs-coinspot/)), Independent Reserve 0.5% ([Independent Reserve](https://www.independentreserve.com/fees)), and Coinbase Advanced 1.20% taker at its entry tier ([DataWallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/coinbase-fees)). Kraken's official page showed Pro spot at 0.40% maker and 0.80% taker in September 2026. That conflicts with its long-standing 0.25%/0.40%, so check it yourself ([Kraken](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule)). The table below turns these fees into the win rate a bot needs just to break even when the stop is 5% away and winners are twice the size of losers. The break-even figures are our own calculation from the listed fees.

| Exchange (entry tier) | Fees for a buy and a sell | Win rate needed to break even (5% stop, 2:1) |
|---|---|---|
| Binance spot | 0.20% | 34.7% |
| BTC Markets (taker) | 0.40% | 36.0% |
| Independent Reserve | 1.00% | 40.0% |
| Swyftx | 1.20% | 41.3% |
| Coinbase Advanced | 2.40% | 49.3% |

At a 2% stop, Swyftx's fees alone would take up 60% of the amount risked on every trade. That is why **Binance spot, or BTC Markets using limit orders that wait on the book, are the only Australian options that can work**. The research notes rank Binance first because it has a free spot test environment ([Binance API](https://www.binance.com/en/binance-api)) and strong Freqtrade support. BTC Markets' Freqtrade support was not confirmed. Binance's reputation in Australia has problems. Its Australian derivatives arm was fined **A$10 million in March 2026** for wrongly classifying retail clients as wholesale ([ASIC 26-055MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-055mr-binance-australia-derivatives-ordered-to-pay-10-million-penalty-for-onboarding-failures-causing-millions-in-client-trading-losses)), and Kraken's Australian operator was fined A$8 million in December 2024 over a margin product ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/296194/kraken-australia-fined-high-risk-margin-trading-products)). Both cases involved leveraged products, not spot trading, which is one more reason to stay on spot. Whether Binance Australia still offers spot API access to new users in 2026 was not verified, so confirm that before building.

**Thailand changes the picture.** The Thai SEC blocked **Bybit, OKX, CoinEx, XT.COM and 1000X from 28 June 2025**, with penalties of up to three years in prison. Binance global was not on that list ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thailand-blocks-okx-bybit-crypto-exchanges); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2025/05/30/thailand-to-block-okx-bybit-and-others-citing-lack-of-license)). If you move, you will generally need to update your exchange identity check (KYC) to your new country of residence. The licensed Thai exchanges (Bitkub and Gulf Binance) were not checked for Freqtrade support. Build the bot so the exchange is a single setting you can change, and re-test fees before switching. While you are an Australian resident, every trade the bot makes is a capital gains tax event, so export the full trade log from Freqtrade.

**Security and running it day to day** are simple but matter. Create an exchange API key (the password-like code the bot uses to place orders) with **trade and read permissions only and withdrawals switched off**. Lock the key to the bot's IP address and keep it out of git and out of the web dashboard. Freqtrade's own docs "strongly recommend" never exposing its API to the internet ([Freqtrade REST API docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/rest-api/)). A US$5 to US$6 a month Linux server running Freqtrade in Docker, with Telegram alerts, is more reliable than a Mac that sleeps, restarts for updates and changes IP address. Also place the stop-loss on the exchange itself (Freqtrade calls this `stoploss_on_exchange`), so your protection survives if the bot goes offline.

## Four gates stand between the backtest and real money

Most impressive backtests are broken before any statistics are run. Freqtrade assumes every order fills at the requested price with no slippage, and it cannot know the order prices moved in within a candle unless you give it lower-timeframe data with `--timeframe-detail`. It says plainly that backtesting "will never replace running a strategy in dry-run mode" ([Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)). **Gate one is a clean backtest.** Use closed candles only, enter at the next candle's open, pass both the lookahead and recursive-analysis checks ([Freqtrade recursive analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/recursive-analysis/)), charge taker fees plus 0.05 to 0.1% slippage per side, and use data from 2017 onwards so the test covers at least two bull markets and two bear markets.

**Gate two is honest comparison.** The more versions you try, the better the best one looks by pure luck. Worse, a strategy fitted too closely to past data can be expected to **lose money on new data, not just break even** ([Bailey et al., AMS Notices](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)). The penalty for testing many versions grows unevenly, so a flat "halve the Sharpe" rule of thumb is "a serious mistake" ([Harvey & Liu](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/backtesting.pdf)). Keep a written log of every version tried. Use round-number settings rather than tuned ones. Check that performance holds when each setting moves 20 to 30% either way ([Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/parameter-optimisation-for-systematic-trading/)). Then compare the bot, after fees, against two benchmarks: buy-and-hold, and "hold BTC only when it is above its 200-day average". If the bot does not beat the 200-day rule on return per unit of drawdown, the extra complexity is not worth it. Your earlier bot would have failed this gate.

**Gate three is new data.** Test in rolling windows, tuning on one stretch of history and testing on the next. If the chosen settings jump around from window to window, the tuning is chasing noise ([Bailey et al., SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253)). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts for how many versions you tried and for crypto's extreme price swings ([Bailey & López de Prado, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)). A tool for estimating the probability that a backtest is overfit is freely available ([pypbo](https://github.com/esvhd/pypbo)). A daily bot makes few trades, so its results will stay statistically uncertain. Running the same rules on ETH as a second check helps.

**Gate four is paper trading.** Run dry-run for at least 4 to 8 weeks **and** at least 30 to 50 trades, whichever comes later. With a daily system, 30 trades can take months, so the 8-week point is really a check that the machinery works, not proof that the strategy works. Paper trading catches broken code, fill and fee mismatches, and hidden lookahead bugs. The test is simple: the live signals should match a fresh backtest over the same weeks at least 95% of the time. The thresholds in this section are standard practitioner practice, not figures from a source.

## Expect crash protection, not income

The best independent result for "AI" trading of BTC, an XGBoost model tested properly on eight years of hourly data, reached a **Sharpe of 1.09 with a 67% drawdown and no statistically significant advantage over buy-and-hold** ([Bysik & Ślepaczuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00060)). Trend rules can hold up after costs, but their proven benefit is avoiding crashes, not higher returns. The authors of the strongest positive study note it "can generate profits in the future only if the market continues to grow" ([Han et al.](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). On $200, even a 20% yearly edge is $40, less than a year of server costs. What a well-built bot gives you is a system that steps aside in bear markets, a working tool, and real experience. It is not a second income.

Studies of human traders point the same way. In Taiwan, **fewer than 1% of day traders were predictably profitable** ([Barber et al., RAPS](https://academic.oup.com/raps/article/10/1/61/5545095)). In Brazil, **97% of those who kept going for more than 300 days lost money** ([Chague et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). ASIC now names "AI trading bots claiming to generate passive income" as a leading scam approach, and Australians lost $837.7 million to investment scams in 2025 ([ASIC 26-063MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-063mr-asic-ramps-up-action-to-protect-consumers-from-ai-powered-online-investment-scams)). The US regulator warns that AI "cannot predict future market movements" ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8854-24)). The research found no independent, exchange-verified track record for Troy Harris's AI Trader app or its six-dot method. Until one exists, treat its marketed results as unproven. Borrowing its display and its discipline is fine. Borrowing its implied returns is not.

## Recommended build plan

**Strategy (long-only spot BTC/USDT, direction from daily candles, entry timing from 4-hour candles):**

| Dot | Rule | Role | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily close above its 100-day moving average | Core trend | Strong |
| 2 | 28-day return above zero | Core momentum | Strong |
| 3 | Price in the upper half of at least 2 of the 20/55/100-day Donchian channels | Core breakout ensemble | Moderate to strong |
| 4 | Weekly close above its 20-week average (or MVRV Z-score not euphoric) | Slow regime filter | Moderate |
| 5 | 4-hour RSI dipped below 40 and turned back up | Entry timing only | Weak |
| 6 | Daily ATR% below its 90th percentile for the past year | Volatility check, also sets size | Moderate as a risk tool |

**Buy** when all six dots are green. **Sell everything** when two of dots 1 to 3 turn red, or when the trailing stop is hit, whichever comes first. **The stop** starts at 2.5 to 3 times daily ATR below entry and only ever moves up. There is **no TP1/TP2/TP3 ladder**. If you want one partial sale, test a single one-third sale at twice the amount risked (2R) against no partial sale, and keep it only if it wins on new data. **Size** each trade so a stop-out loses 1 to 2% of the account. Use **no leverage, no shorting and no futures**.

**Setup:** Freqtrade in Docker on a US$5 to US$6 a month Linux server, with Telegram alerts, the stop-loss held on the exchange, and your dashboard reading Freqtrade's API through FastAPI. The exchange is Binance spot, after confirming Australian API access, with BTC Markets using limit orders as the Australian fallback. Re-check the exchange before you move to Thailand. The API key is trade-only, with withdrawals off and locked to the server's IP, in a sub-account holding only the bot's money.

**Validation gates (each must pass before moving on):**

| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| 1. Clean backtest | Data from 2017 onwards, fees of 0.1% per side plus 0.05 to 0.1% slippage, lookahead and recursive checks clean, `--timeframe-detail` used |
| 2. Benchmarks | Beats both buy-and-hold and the 200-day rule on return per unit of drawdown after fees; stable when each setting moves 20 to 30%; every version tried is logged |
| 3. New data | Tuned-period and test-period results broadly agree across rolling windows; Deflated Sharpe probability at least 0.95; similar behaviour on ETH |
| 4. Paper trading | At least 8 weeks and 30 trades, 95% or better signal match with a fresh backtest, no unhandled errors, fees and fills as expected |
| 5. Live | Start at the smallest allowed position size, and increase only after each 30 live trades stay within the backtest's expected range |

**Stop rules, decided now:** switch the bot off if the live drawdown passes 1.5 times the backtest's maximum drawdown. Also switch it off if it trails the 200-day rule benchmark over any six months, or if paper or live signals stop matching the backtest. Never add money to win back losses. If the bot fails gate 2, the right move is to hold BTC with the 200-day rule, or simply hold it outright.

## Conclusion

The six-dot idea is not wrong, but it does something different from what the app suggests. Requiring many dots to agree works by cutting the number of trades, which cuts fees. It does not find hidden opportunities. So the useful design question is not which six indicators are cleverest, but which few genuinely different signals keep the bot out of the market most of the time and out of every major crash. Your failed backtest is the most valuable result so far, because it showed, with your own money model, that fees and trading speed matter more than the choice of indicators.

The honest benchmark for this project is the 200-day rule, not buy-and-hold. If a carefully validated six-dot bot cannot beat a single moving-average rule after fees, the lesson is that the simplest system was the best one. Learning that for the cost of a small account and some server fees is still a good outcome.
